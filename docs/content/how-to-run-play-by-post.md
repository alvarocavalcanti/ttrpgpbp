---
title: How to Run a Play-by-Post Game
---

Running a play-by-post campaign is game mastering at a different tempo. You trade the energy of a live table for a story that builds in writing, day by day. Done well, it is one of the most rewarding ways to run a long campaign. Here is how to set one up and keep it alive.

## Recruit the right table

A play-by-post group needs people who will actually post. When you invite players, be honest about the rhythm:

- Say how often you expect posts — for example, **once a day on weekdays**.
- Say how long the campaign is likely to run.
- Ask what they want from the game: heavy roleplay, tactical combat, exploration, or a mix.

Even three committed players beat six who drift away.

## Set the ground rules up front

A short session zero does a lot of work over text. Cover:

- **Pace** — when everyone posts, and how combat turns work.
- **Safety tools** — fill in **lines and veils** and explain the **anonymous X-card** so anyone can flag a moment without speaking up.
- **Tone and content** — what kind of story this is, and what is out of bounds.
- **House rules** — which system you are playing and any table conventions.

Role by Post keeps the first two in the channel itself, so they are always one tap away.

## Structure your scenes

Without a session clock, scenes can drift. Give each one a shape:

1. **Open** with a prompt that states where the party is and what is at stake.
2. **Play** — let players post actions and dialogue; answer with replies so threads stay readable.
3. **Close** when the objective is met or the scene has run its course, then open the next with a scene break.

Not every scene needs combat. Downtime, travel, and conversation are where character moments live in this format.

## Run combat with clear turns

Combat is the part that most needs structure, because it is the only place strict order matters.

- Put **initiative** and the **active player** on the status bar.
- Announce each turn plainly and name who is up next.
- Remind players they can tap dice notation to roll, or open an [ability check](/help/ability-checks) with their modifier already filled in.
- If a player is away, use **AFK status** and either skip, delay, or take their turn for them — but decide the rule before it comes up.

## Play the world, not just the plot

Play-by-post shines when the world feels alive between the main beats.

- Give **NPCs** names and portraits and let them speak in their own voice.
- Let players pursue personal goals in side scenes.
- Use the **status bar** for ongoing notes — a countdown, a riddle, a promise the party made.

## Keep the campaign moving

- **Post every day you promised.** A Game Master who goes quiet teaches the table that silence is fine.
- **Recap often.** A short summary at the top of a scene helps anyone who fell behind.
- **Ask directly.** "Kira, what does your character do?" brings a quiet player back in.
- **Export the story.** Save the campaign to Markdown now and then; it is a chronicle worth keeping.

## Get started
