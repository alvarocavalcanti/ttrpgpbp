RPOL (RolePlay onLine) has hosted play-by-post roleplaying since 2000, and it is still busy: thousands of games on record, millions of posts, and a community that has kept the format alive for decades. If you want a classic forum game with a deep bench of tables to join, it remains a real option — you can [look at RPOL yourself](https://rpol.net).

Role by Post is a newer take on the same idea, built for how people play today. This page is an honest comparison, including the things RPOL does better.

<!-- shots -->

## At a glance

- **A chat-first timeline**, so a campaign reads back like a novel instead of a stack of threads.
- **Up to ten rooms per campaign**, each with its own status bar and privacy, so dice and out-of-character talk stay out of the story.
- **A character in every campaign** — its own name, portrait, and optional character sheet link.
- **Installable and free**, with push alerts you can tune to just your turn.

## Where RPOL is still the right home

Credit where it is due. These are the reasons people stay, and none of them is a small thing:

- **A catalogue nothing else matches.** RPOL reports 4,400+ games, over 9 million posts, and around 141 games recruiting players at any time, with roughly eight new games created every day. If you want to join a game tonight, that depth matters.
- **A portrait library and hosted sheets.** More than 14,000 portraits, character sheets hosted on the site, game maps, private threads, and groups for keeping parts of a game to some players.
- **One login, many characters.** A strict one-account policy, with unlimited anonymous character aliases inside each game, so a decade of characters lives behind a single sign-in.
- **It is free**, and has been funded by its community for over two decades.

If a large public directory and forum-style boards are what you want, RPOL is built exactly for that, and this page will not talk you out of it.

## Where Role by Post differs

### The story reads like a novel, not a thread

A campaign is one flowing timeline rather than a set of boards. Posts keep their replies, reactions, and scene breaks attached to the moment they belong to, the timeline marks where you last read, and full-text search jumps you straight to any line. When the campaign ends you can **export the whole chat history to Markdown** and keep it.

### Rooms keep side talk out of the story

Every campaign starts with **Main**, and the Game Master can add up to ten **rooms** beside it: one for out-of-character chatter, one for dice, one for rules questions, or a private room for a single player's side scene. Messages, replies, and rolls stay in the room they were posted in, each room keeps its own status bar and unread dot, and dice and NPC posts can be switched on or off per room — so the story room stays prose. [How rooms work](/help/rooms)

### A character in every campaign

One account holds a different character in each game. Each campaign gives you its own **character name**, its own **portrait**, and an optional **character sheet link**, so the rogue you play on Tuesdays and the paladin in the weekend game do not share a profile. The name and portrait replace your account identity only inside that campaign — messages, the member list, mentions, and the active-player picker all show the character the table knows. [How characters work](/help/account)

### Game tools inside the conversation

Roll straight from a post: tap dice notation to roll, open an [ability check](/help/ability-checks) with the modifier pre-filled, or roll pools and success dice for systems that read each die on its own. Game Masters speak as **NPCs** with their own names and portraits, a persistent status bar holds initiative and notes, and **[safety tools](/help/safety-tools)** — lines and veils plus an anonymous X-card — sit inside each channel instead of in a separate document.

### Notes that live with the campaign

Three notebooks per campaign: **Character Notes** that stay between you and the Game Master, **Party Notes** everyone shares, and **GM Notes** only the Game Master reads. They are saved with the campaign rather than in a separate document nobody can find. [Campaign notes](/help/notes)

### It feels like an app

Role by Post is mobile-first and installable, so a campaign opens like a native chat app rather than a browser tab full of boards. Unread counts, a new-messages divider, and **per-channel push notifications** keep the table moving — set to everything, Game Master posts only, or just when it is your turn. An AFK status pauses your alerts when you step away.

## Questions people ask

### Is there an RPOL app for phones?

RPOL runs in a browser; it does not ship an installable app. Role by Post can be installed to your home screen and sends push notifications, including an alert when it is your turn — which is the part that keeps an asynchronous game moving on a phone.

### Can I bring my RPOL games over to Role by Post?

There is no importer, and pretending otherwise would waste your time. What you can do is keep your RPOL account, start a new campaign on Role by Post for a fresh story, and paste across anything you want to reuse. Your own writing is yours; the export tools here are for campaigns played on this site.

### Do I have to give up RPOL?

No. Some tables want a big public directory and forum boards, and RPOL does that well. Role by Post is the better fit when you want the story to read like a chat, to play on a phone, and to keep the game's tools and safety settings inside the campaign. Groups also run campaigns in both places.

### What does Role by Post cost?

Nothing. It is free to play, with no paid tier gating rooms, dice, notes, or notifications.

### Is this only for Dungeons & Dragons?

No. Games are played here across many systems, including a dedicated [Shadowdark](/game-systems/shadowdark) setup with its own character rules. The dice roller handles sum rolls, pools, and success dice, so systems that read each die on its own work too.
