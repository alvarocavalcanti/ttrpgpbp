---
title: Role by Post vs RPOL — A Modern Play-by-Post Alternative
---

RPOL (RolePlay OnLine) has hosted play-by-post games for decades. It is a long-running, text-first platform with a deep bench of games and a community that knows the format well. If you are looking for a classic forum-style play-by-post home, it has real history behind it.

Role by Post is a newer take on the same idea, built for how people play today. Here is how the two compare.

## The format

Both are asynchronous: you post, the game waits, and the story grows in text. If the classic forum style is what you want — threaded boards, a wide catalogue of games, and a long track record — RPOL is built exactly for that.

Role by Post arranges the same play as a **chat-first timeline**. It is a single flowing story per campaign rather than a forum thread, and it is designed to read well on a phone — which is where most players actually post from.

## A character in every campaign

One account holds a different character in each game. Every channel gives you its own **character name**, its own **portrait**, and an optional **character sheet link**, so the rogue you play on Tuesdays and the paladin in the weekend game do not share a profile. The name and portrait replace your account identity only inside that campaign — messages, the member list, mentions, and the active-player picker all show the character the table knows. [Edit Character](/help/account) sits one tap away in the member list.

## Rooms keep the story clean

Every campaign starts with **Main**, and the GM can add up to ten **rooms** beside it: one for out-of-character chatter, one for dice, one for rules questions, or a private room for a single player. Messages, replies, and dice stay in the room they were posted in, each room keeps its own **status bar** and its own **unread dot**, and a private room only appears for the players the GM invited. The GM can also switch dice, scene breaks, NPC posts, and whispers on or off per room, so dice and NPC posts stay in the rooms that need them and the story room stays prose. [How rooms work](/help/rooms)

## Mobile and feel

This is the biggest practical difference.

- RPOL is a traditional web application; its interface is dense and built around desktop use.
- Role by Post is **mobile-first and installable**, so a campaign feels like a native app. Messages, replies, and reactions are laid out for a small screen, and push notifications arrive like a chat app.

## Game tools

Role by Post puts the table's tools inside the conversation:

- A native **[dice roller](/help/dice-rolling)** — tap notation in a post to roll, open an [ability check](/help/ability-checks) with the modifier pre-filled, and roll pools or success dice.
- **NPCs** with names and portraits so the Game Master can speak as a character and keep that identity in the history.
- A persistent **status bar** for initiative, active players, and notes.
- **[Safety tools](/help/safety-tools)** — lines and veils plus an anonymous X-card — built into each channel.
- **Unread counts**, a new-messages divider, and **per-channel push notifications** that can be set to ping you only when it is your turn.
- **Campaign notes** — character notes that stay between you and the GM, party notes everyone shares, and GM-only notes, all saved with the campaign instead of a separate document.
- **Whispers** — a private aside to the Game Master inside the same timeline, instead of a direct message the story never sees.
- **Full-text search** and a **Markdown export** of the whole campaign.

## The short version

| | RPOL | Role by Post |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Style | Classic forum play-by-post | Chat-first timeline |
| Finding a game | Decades of games to join | Bring your group, or browse listed games in Discover |
| Character per campaign | Per-game characters | Name, portrait, and sheet link per campaign |
| Rooms | Forum boards and threads | Up to 10 rooms, with private rooms and per-room status |
| Campaign notes | Forum threads and sheets | Character, party, and GM notebooks |
| Mobile experience | Traditional web UI | Mobile-first, installable app |
| Dice | Built in | Built in, with ability checks and pools |
| Turn alerts | Manual | Per-channel push, including "your turn" |
| Safety tools | Community norms | Built into the channel |
| Cost | Free | Free |

## The bottom line

RPOL is a piece of play-by-post history and still a fine home for forum-style games. If what you want is a **modern, mobile-first, chat-like** place to play asynchronously — with rooms, a character for every campaign, turn alerts, and game tools built in — [Role by Post](/features) is made for that. It is free and you can start a campaign in minutes. New to the format? Read [what play-by-post is](/play-by-post).
